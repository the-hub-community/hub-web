# Beginner Photography Basics

<div class="lh-content-body">

<div class="lh-section">


<div class="lh-text">
Getting into photography can feel overwhelming. There are endless buttons, menus, and lenses. However, the secret to taking great photos is surprisingly simple: mastering the Exposure Triangle. This consists of three settings: Aperture, Shutter Speed, and ISO.

Aperture is the opening in your lens. It controls how much light comes in and, more importantly, your depth of field. A low f-number (like f/1.8 or f/2.8) creates a blurry background (bokeh), which is great for portraits. A high f-number (like f/8 or f/11) keeps everything sharp from front to back, which is ideal for landscapes.

Shutter Speed is how fast your camera’s shutter opens and closes. If you want to freeze the action of a running dog, use a fast shutter speed (like 1/1000th of a second). If you want to capture the smooth blur of a waterfall, use a slow shutter speed (like 1/4th of a second) and place your camera on a tripod to avoid camera shake.

ISO is your camera’s sensitivity to light. In bright daylight, keep it low (ISO 100) to avoid grainy “noise” in your photos. In dark environments, raising your ISO (to 800 or 1600) allows you to capture the shot, though it will add some grain.

Composition is just as important as settings. Always ask yourself where the light is coming from. If the sun is behind your subject, they will be a silhouette unless you use a flash or reflector. Follow the “Rule of Thirds”: imagine a grid dividing your photo into nine squares and place your subject on one of the intersecting lines rather than dead center.

Start by shooting in “Aperture Priority” mode (A or Av on your camera dial). This lets you control the background blur while the camera handles the shutter speed. Practice every day, and your eye will improve quickly.
